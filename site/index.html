After 5pm and on Saturdays, about half of Tulsa's highest-need neighborhoods can't reach an open pantry without a car.

On a weekday, 95% of people in the county's 52 highest-need tracts can walk or ride the bus to an open food pantry within 30 minutes. After 5pm it's 54%. On a Saturday, 50%.

When the pantries are open

The pantry network covers Tulsa's highest-need neighborhoods well on a weekday: pantries cluster where need is highest, and so do bus routes. Of the 69 general-public pantries in the county, 53 keep weekday daytime hours.

After work and on weekends it thins out. 17 are open on a weekday evening and 12 on a Saturday (8 of those every Saturday). The buses thin out too: at a typical stop one comes every 47 minutes on a weekday, every 60 after 5pm.

In the high-need tracts, the number of people in poverty with no open pantry within reach goes from about 2,200 on a weekday to about 21,000 on an evening and about 23,000 on a Saturday: about ten times as many. A pantry open only 10 to 2 on weekdays does not help a parent who works those hours.

The clearest case is the tract around E 61st St & Peoria Ave: 47% of residents below the poverty line and 28% of households without a car. On a weekday 2 pantries are open within a mile. After 5pm the nearest open one is 7.2 km away, on a Saturday 5.0 km, and no part of the tract can reach one in 30 minutes.

P.S. These numbers assume riders check the schedule. Someone who just walks to the stop waits half the time between buses; then only 19% of high-need residents reach an open pantry on a Saturday.

How this was worked out

Need

An equal blend of three tract shares from the ACS 2020-2024: people below the poverty line, households without a car, households on SNAP. "High need" is the top quarter of the county's 208 tracts (52 tracts, 154,111 people).

Pantries

The Food Finder's general-public pantries and their regular sessions, including "2nd & 4th Saturday" rules. A pantry counts in a window if a session overlaps it by 30 minutes. City, ZIP and school-district limits are applied.

Getting there

From every populated census block: walking at 3.0 mph, or walking to a stop, riding (one transfer at most) and walking on, all within 30 minutes, on MetroLink's published schedule. A rider who checks the schedule waits 5 minutes for the first bus.

Checked

  • Totals add up. Tract populations sum to the county (680,794), and the 2020 census blocks used as trip origins sum to the official 2020 count (669,279).
  • Three tracts recomputed by hand. From the raw Census rows, the highest-, median- and lowest-need tracts match exactly. Tract 10: 416 of 1299 people below poverty, 330 of 711 households without a car.
  • USDA agrees. Its own tract tabulation correlates 1.00 (poverty), 0.98 (no car) and 0.99 (SNAP) with these figures.
  • Bus trips checked against the timetable. In 300 sampled trips, the best one-bus trip found straight from the schedule never beat the model by more than 3 minutes, and gave the same reachable-or-not answer in 100%.
  • A real trip planner agrees. R5, routing on the street network and the full timetable for 500 sample blocks, gives the same reachable-or-not answer for 94% (weekday day), 93% (weekday evening), 95% (saturday) of residents. The shortcut runs 1 to 3 points optimistic.

What could be wrong

  • Survey margins. Tract figures come from a sample, so every rate on the map shows its 90% margin. 1 of the 19 tracts on any top-ten list has a measure with a wide margin.
  • Hours go stale. Pantries post their own hours, and they change. 7 county pantries list none. Counting all of those as open in every window lifts the Saturday share in high-need tracts from 50% to 73%, an upper bound.
  • The bus is a model. Scheduled service only, from the feed valid Sep 10, 2026 to Dec 5, 2026. No late buses, no trip home, no groceries to carry, and walking is measured in straight lines.
  • Tracts are averages. Need is a tract average and hides pockets inside better-off tracts. Access is worked out block by block, but those block populations date from 2020.
  • Not every pantry. Only the public Food Finder's listings: school, senior and meal programs are left out, and pantries outside the Food Bank's network are missing.
