Tulsa Pantry Gap Map · Brief

By Taylor Burke · November 8, 2026 · A personal project from public data, not a product of the Food Bank of Eastern Oklahoma

After 5pm and on Saturdays, about half of Tulsa's highest-need neighborhoods can't reach an open pantry without a car.

Tulsa County's 52 highest-need census tracts are home to 154,000 people, 46,000 of them below the poverty line. Walking and riding the bus, 30 minutes door to door, almost all of them can get to an open food pantry on a weekday. Most pantries close by 5pm and few open on Saturday, so about ten times as many people in poverty are left without one on a Saturday as on a weekday.

How it's measured

Trips start from every 2020 census block: walking at 3 mph (straight-line distance × 1.3) plus scheduled Tulsa Transit service with at most one transfer, from the published timetable. Riders who check the schedule wait 5 minutes for the first bus. A full trip planner (R5) gives the same reachable-or-not answer for 93%–95% of residents. Supply is every general-public pantry on the Food Bank's public Food Finder, with its posted hours; school, senior-only and meal programs are left out, and service-area rules are applied where a listing states them.

Maps show the Tulsa urban area, which holds all 52 high-need tracts. High-need: the top quarter of county tracts on poverty, households without a car and SNAP use (2020–24 American Community Survey). Pantries: general-public pantries on the Food Bank's public Food Finder with a regular session in the window.

Where a mobile distribution would help most

A first screening, not a recommendation. These are the bus stops where one mobile distribution would put the most people in poverty who have no open pantry today within 30 minutes of one, picked one at a time so each adds to the ones before it. It doesn't weigh parking, host sites, capacity or meal programs. They're the numbered squares on the maps.

What it can't tell you

  • Survey margins. Tract figures come from a sample, so every rate on the map shows its 90% margin. 1 of the 19 tracts on any top-ten list has a measure with a wide margin.
  • Hours go stale. Pantries post their own hours, and they change. 7 county pantries list none. Counting all of those as open in every window lifts the Saturday share in high-need tracts from 50% to 73%, an upper bound.
  • The bus is a model. Scheduled service only, from the feed valid Sep 10, 2026 to Dec 5, 2026. No late buses, no trip home, no groceries to carry, and walking is measured in straight lines.
  • Tracts are averages. Need is a tract average and hides pockets inside better-off tracts. Access is worked out block by block, but those block populations date from 2020.
  • Not every pantry. Only the public Food Finder's listings: school, senior and meal programs are left out, and pantries outside the Food Bank's network are missing.
